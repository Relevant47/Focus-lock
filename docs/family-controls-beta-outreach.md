## Notes for sending

- **Tailor the "[reason]"** to each recipient — generic outreach reads as marketing. "You mentioned struggling with your kid's screen time last month" lands; "I thought you might be interested" doesn't.
- **Five is enough.** A bigger beta means more noise and slower iteration. The point is finding sharp edges, not validating that the thing works.
- **Don't ask for feedback up front.** Ask for participation. Feedback comes after they've actually used it.
- **Set the expectation that you'll bother them.** "I'll check in after a week to ask what broke" makes it feel collaborative; silence after handoff makes them think the beta was a chore.
- **Take notes on what you DON'T see.** If three families set up and then never mention it again, that's data too — it's quietly not working.
